4-7-8 Breathing: How to Do It, What It Does, and What the Research Shows

Quick answer

4-7-8 breathing is a slow breathing exercise: breathe in quietly through your nose for 4 counts, hold your breath for 7, then breathe out through your mouth with a soft whoosh for 8. Dr. Andrew Weil popularised it as the “Relaxing Breath”, and it has roots in the yogic practice of pranayama. It is a simple way to wind down, though research on 4-7-8 itself is still small.

How do you do 4-7-8 breathing?

These are the steps from Dr. Weil’s patient handout. Do them as one smooth sequence.

  1. Sit with your back straight while you learn the exercise. Weil says you can do it in any position, and many people lie down at bedtime.
  2. Place the tip of your tongue against the ridge of tissue just behind your upper front teeth, and keep it there the whole time.
  3. Breathe out completely through your mouth, around your tongue, making a whoosh sound. Pursing your lips slightly helps if this feels awkward.
  4. Close your mouth and breathe in quietly through your nose to a mental count of 4.
  5. Hold your breath for a count of 7.
  6. Breathe out completely through your mouth, with a whoosh, to a count of 8.
  7. That is one breath. Breathe in again and repeat the cycle three more times, for four breaths in total.

Do the 4-7-8 counts have to be seconds?

No. The counts are a ratio, not a fixed number of seconds. Weil’s handout says the time spent on each phase is not important, but the ratio of 4:7:8 is. If you struggle to hold your breath, speed up the whole exercise and keep the same ratio. With practice you can slow everything down. Many websites write the counts as seconds, but the ratio is the part to keep.

How often should you practise 4-7-8 breathing?

  • Do it at least twice a day. Weil says you cannot do it too frequently.
  • For the first month, do no more than four breaths at one time. Later, if you wish, you can extend it to eight breaths.
  • Use it when you notice tension, before you react to something upsetting, and to help you fall asleep.
  • Practise when you feel calm too, not only in a difficult moment.

How does 4-7-8 breathing work?

Nobody has tested exactly why 4-7-8 helps, so these explanations come from research on slow breathing in general.

  • The long exhale. Heart rate rises slightly as you breathe in and falls as you breathe out. Vagus nerve activity, the main nerve of the body’s calming “rest and digest” system, is higher during exhaling and slow breathing. In 4-7-8 the exhale is twice as long as the inhale.
  • The hold. One review lists proposed, not proven, reasons: a slow pace of about 3 breaths per minute, a low inhale-to-exhale ratio, and possibly less chemoreceptor stimulation during the hold.
  • Counting gives your mind a job. Dr. Young says the counting is “a way to focus your mind on something other than your worries”.
  • Practice matters. Weil says the effect is subtle at first and gains in power with repetition and practice.

Does 4-7-8 breathing work? What the research shows

Honestly: the studies on 4-7-8 are few, small and mostly short. Medical News Today notes there is limited clinical research, and a 2025 review lists only two 4-7-8 studies. Here is what has been found:

  • After surgery. In a randomised trial of 90 bariatric surgery patients, the 4-7-8 group had lower anxiety afterwards than both the deep-breathing group and the control group.
  • Tinnitus and sleep. In a small randomised trial (23 vs 25 people analysed), 4 cycles twice daily for 6 weeks lowered the 4-7-8 group’s Insomnia Severity Index score from 8.39 to 4.35. The control group did not change significantly. The groups differed in anxiety at the start, and participants knew which group they were in.
  • Head to head. In a study of 84 students, “breathing at 6 bpm increased HRV measures more than square or 4-7-8 breathing.” No technique changed blood pressure or mood meaningfully.

The evidence for slow breathing in general is stronger. A meta-analysis of randomised trials found that breathwork lowered stress compared with control (g = −0.35, 12 trials, 785 people), though most studies had a moderate risk of bias.

Bottom line: it is a reasonable relaxation habit, but it is not proven to be better than other slow-breathing methods.

Can 4-7-8 breathing help you fall asleep?

Weil’s handout says: “Use it to help you fall asleep.” Insomnia scores improved in the tinnitus trial above, but none of the studies we found measured how long it takes to fall asleep with objective tools such as sleep-lab recordings.

What about “fall asleep in 60 seconds”? That claim is not supported. It comes from 2015 press headlines, including a HuffPost UK piece that cited no study, and it does not appear in Weil’s own handout. If each count is one second, four breaths take about 76 seconds, so “60 seconds” most likely describes how long the exercise lasts rather than how fast sleep arrives.

A better way to use it: make it part of a bedtime wind-down, lying down, and give it a few weeks. If insomnia is frequent or long-lasting, Healthline advises seeing a doctor. More ideas are in our guide to breathing exercises for sleep.

Is 4-7-8 breathing good for anxiety?

It may help some people feel calmer. The bariatric surgery trial above found lower anxiety with 4-7-8, and in a trial of 75 older adults before an endoscopy, 4-7-8 reduced fear and anxiety, although a stress ball reduced anxiety more. As with any breathing exercise, it works best as one tool among others: Cleveland Clinic says breathing exercises should not be the only treatment for anxiety.

If you want something for sudden, intense anxiety, see breathing for panic attacks.

4-7-8 breathing vs box breathing

  • 4-7-8: in 4, hold 7, out 8. The exhale is twice as long as the inhale. WebMD links it with relaxation.
  • Box breathing: in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. Cleveland Clinic notes US Navy SEALs use it, and WebMD says it “is said to also help improve concentration”.
  • Which is better? Neither is proven better for mood. In the only head-to-head HRV study we found, 6 breaths per minute beat both.
  • If you dislike holding your breath: the NHS describes a calm breathing exercise with no hold: in for 1 to 5, out for 1 to 5, for at least 5 minutes.

Common 4-7-8 breathing mistakes

  • Treating the counts as fixed seconds. The ratio is what matters.
  • Doing too many rounds too soon. Stay at four breaths for the first month.
  • Breathing through the wrong route. Breathe in quietly through your nose and out audibly through your mouth.
  • Straining on the hold. Cleveland Clinic says to hold “without straining”.
  • Expecting instant results. Medical News Today says benefits may show “after several days or weeks”.

Is 4-7-8 breathing safe?

  • Mild lightheadedness is common at first. Weil says it will pass. Cleveland Clinic notes beginners may also feel shortness of breath.
  • Sit or lie down. Stop or shorten the breaths if you feel dizzy, and take a break.
  • If the hold is uncomfortable, speed up the counts but keep the 4:7:8 ratio.
  • Breath-hold techniques may not suit people who find them difficult. Medical News Today advises people with high blood pressure, or who are pregnant, to speak with a doctor before box breathing, and Cleveland Clinic says people with COPD, asthma or anxiety should talk to their provider about breathing exercises.

Not medical advice

This page is general information, not medical advice. Breathing exercises are not a treatment for a medical condition. Speak to a doctor or qualified professional if you have a health condition, if your anxiety or sleep problems are frequent or severe, or if you ever feel unwell while practising. If you think you are having a medical emergency, contact your local emergency services.

4-7-8 breathing myths vs facts

Myth: It puts you to sleep in 60 seconds.

Fact: No study we found has measured this. The claim comes from 2015 headlines.

Myth: The counts must be seconds.

Fact: Weil says the 4:7:8 ratio matters, not the seconds.

Myth: It is scientifically proven.

Fact: Research on 4-7-8 is limited and small.

4-7-8 breathing: frequently asked questions

What is 4-7-8 breathing?

A slow breathing exercise: breathe in through your nose for 4 counts, hold for 7, and breathe out through your mouth for 8. Dr. Andrew Weil popularised it as the Relaxing Breath.

How do you do 4-7-8 breathing?

Rest your tongue behind your upper front teeth, breathe out with a whoosh, then breathe in through your nose for 4, hold for 7 and breathe out through your mouth for 8. Do four breaths in total.

Does 4-7-8 breathing actually work?

Small studies suggest it can help people relax, but research on 4-7-8 itself is limited. Evidence for slow breathing in general is stronger.

Can 4-7-8 breathing make you fall asleep in 60 seconds?

No study we found has measured that claim. It comes from 2015 press headlines, and Dr. Weil’s own handout says the effect is subtle at first and grows with practice.

How many times a day should I do 4-7-8 breathing?

Dr. Weil says at least twice a day. For the first month, do no more than four breaths at a time. After that you can extend it to eight breaths.

Do the 4-7-8 counts have to be seconds?

No. Dr. Weil says the 4:7:8 ratio matters, not the seconds. If the hold is hard, speed up the whole exercise and keep the same ratio.

Is 4-7-8 breathing safe, and why might I feel lightheaded?

Mild lightheadedness is common at first, and Dr. Weil says it passes. Sit or lie down, do not strain on the hold, and stop if you feel dizzy. If you have a health condition or are pregnant, check with your doctor first.
