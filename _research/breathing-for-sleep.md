# Research: /breathing-for-sleep/

Research date: 2026-11-08. Source links use [Sn] labels that match the numbered Sources list.

## Summary

- Slow breathing before bed probably helps by lowering "presleep arousal", the body and mind being too switched on to sleep ([S21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7198497/), [S24](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6361823/)). The direct evidence is small. A 2026 systematic review (9 studies, 457 participants) found that people *reported* better sleep, but the objective measurements were inconclusive ([S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41886931/)).
- The protocol studied most is slow paced breathing at about 6 breaths per minute (0.1 Hz), done for 15–30 minutes before bed, every night for about 4 weeks ([S19](https://hub.tmu.edu.tw/en/publications/efficacy-of-paced-breathing-for-insomnia-enhances-vagal-activity-/), [S20](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6406675/), [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41886931/)).
- Breathing counts as a type of "relaxation therapy". The AASM recommends relaxation therapy only conditionally, based on low-quality evidence. CBT-I is the strong, first-line recommendation for chronic insomnia ([S16](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7853203/), [S17](https://www.healio.com/internal-medicine/sleep-medicine/news/online/%7B366103d7-dcd1-47d1-8795-90dc3281e2b4%7D/acp-recommends-cognitive-behavioral-therapy-for-chronic-insomnia)).
- Waking briefly in the night is normal. If you are awake, sources suggest slow breathing in bed, not checking the clock, and getting up after about 15–20 minutes ([S38](https://www.rte.ie/brainstorm/2026/0421/1569138-sleep-3am-insomnia-health/), [S3](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/how-to-fall-back-asleep), [S2](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/insomnia/treatment/relaxation-exercises-to-help-fall-asleep)).
- Most top-ranking pages are listicles from brands or apps. They skip how strong the evidence is, CBT-I, sleep apnoea warning signs, what to do at 3am, and when breathing exercises backfire.

## Why breathing helps sleep

- Presleep arousal is "the mental and physiological activation that individuals experience when trying to fall asleep" ([S21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7198497/)).
- One explanation of insomnia is "hyperarousal", described as a "tired but wired" state. In this view, slow breathing may raise vagal (parasympathetic) tone. The source is a perspective article, so this is a hypothesis, not a proven finding ([S24](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6361823/)).
- People who reported insomnia showed "depressed high frequency power of HRV" compared with good sleepers ([S19](https://hub.tmu.edu.tw/en/publications/efficacy-of-paced-breathing-for-insomnia-enhances-vagal-activity-/)). HRV means heart rate variability.
- Across 15 studies, slow breathing (<10 breaths per minute) was linked to higher HRV, more relaxation and less anxiety. The authors call this evidence "interesting albeit limited" ([S25](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137615/)).
- Cleveland Clinic lists lower heart rate and blood pressure among the benefits of diaphragmatic breathing ([S11](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/9445-diaphragmatic-breathing)).
- Sleep Foundation: "Taking slow, deep breaths is one of the easiest and most basic ways to engage your body's natural relaxation response" ([S2](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/insomnia/treatment/relaxation-exercises-to-help-fall-asleep)).
- Racing thoughts: the AASM defines relaxation therapy as targeting both body tension and "cognitive arousal" ([S16](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7853203/)). Harvard says breath focus means gently shifting attention away from distracting thoughts ([S15](https://www.harvardhealth.org/mind-and-mood/six-relaxation-techniques-to-reduce-stress)).

## Techniques and exact steps

- **Slow paced breathing (~6 breaths per minute).**
  - Laborde 2019: inhale through the nose for 4.5 s, exhale through pursed lips for 5.5 s, for 15 minutes each evening before sleep, for 30 days ([S20](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6406675/)).
  - Tsai 2015: 0.1 Hz for 20 minutes before sleep ([S19](https://hub.tmu.edu.tw/en/publications/efficacy-of-paced-breathing-for-insomnia-enhances-vagal-activity-/)).
  - Kuula 2020: 5 breaths per minute for 30 minutes, done seated about 1 hour before bed ([S21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7198497/)).
  - Simpler NHS version: count 1 to 5 breathing in and 1 to 5 breathing out, without forcing it, for at least 5 minutes. NHS gives this for stress, not specifically for sleep ([S7](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/)).
  - The claim that each person has their own "resonance frequency" is UNVERIFIED.
- **Diaphragmatic (belly) breathing.**
  - Sleep Foundation: one hand on the chest and one on the belly. Breathe in through the nose so the belly rises and the chest stays still. Do 20 breaths ([S1](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/best-breathing-exercises-for-sleep)), or "a few minutes... when you get into bed" ([S2](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/insomnia/treatment/relaxation-exercises-to-help-fall-asleep)).
  - Cleveland Clinic: learn it lying down first. Practise for 5–10 minutes, 3–4 times a day ([S11](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/9445-diaphragmatic-breathing)).
- **Extended exhale.**
  - The cyclic sigh: two inhales through the nose, then a slow, full exhale through the mouth, for 5 minutes a day ([S27](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)).
  - App-style variants: 4-4-8 ([S40](https://www.calm.com/blog/breathing-exercises-for-sleep)) and 4-4-6 ([S42](https://www.headspace.com/sleep/breathing-exercises-for-better-sleep)).
- **4-7-8 (keep this section brief).** Rest the tip of your tongue on the ridge behind your upper front teeth. Exhale with a whoosh. Inhale through the nose for 4, hold for 7, exhale for 8. "Do not do more than four breaths at one time for the first month" ([S32](https://awcim.arizona.edu/content/CLH00048.html)). Sleep Foundation says to repeat about 6 times, which conflicts with this ([S1](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/best-breathing-exercises-for-sleep)).
- **Box breathing.** Inhale 4, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4 ([S1](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/best-breathing-exercises-for-sleep)).
- **Body scan.** After a few deep breaths, move your attention from your feet up to your forehead and release tension in each area ([S2](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/insomnia/treatment/relaxation-exercises-to-help-fall-asleep), [S15](https://www.harvardhealth.org/mind-and-mood/six-relaxation-techniques-to-reduce-stress)).
- **Progressive muscle relaxation (PMR).** Tense each muscle group for 5–10 seconds, then pause for 10–20 seconds before the next group ([S2](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/insomnia/treatment/relaxation-exercises-to-help-fall-asleep)). Harvard's version: lie on your back, take slow breaths, and work upward from the feet ([S14](https://www.harvardhealth.org/sleep/progressive-relaxation-techniques-for-insomnia)).
- **Others.** Alternate nostril breathing, Bhramari ([S1](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/best-breathing-exercises-for-sleep)), and counting breaths ([S40](https://www.calm.com/blog/breathing-exercises-for-sleep)). No sleep-specific evidence was found for these.

## Benefits and evidence

| Claim | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| CBT-I treats chronic insomnia | AASM strong recommendation [S16](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7853203/). ACP: strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence [S17](https://www.healio.com/internal-medicine/sleep-medicine/news/online/%7B366103d7-dcd1-47d1-8795-90dc3281e2b4%7D/acp-recommends-cognitive-behavioral-therapy-for-chronic-insomnia). "7 to 8 out of 10 people show significant improvement" [S10](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/cognitive-behavioral-therapy-insomnia) | Strong |
| Relaxation therapy (includes abdominal breathing and PMR) helps insomnia | "a small body of low quality evidence from 5 studies"; conditional recommendation [S16](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7853203/) | Low |
| Slow breathing before bed improves sleep | Systematic review of 9 studies (457 participants): self-reported sleep improved; 2 actigraphy and 3 polysomnography studies were inconclusive [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41886931/) | Preliminary |
| Slow breathing in insomnia (Tsai 2015) | 14 people with self-reported insomnia: shorter time to fall asleep, fewer awakenings, higher sleep efficiency [S19](https://hub.tmu.edu.tw/en/publications/efficacy-of-paced-breathing-for-insomnia-enhances-vagal-activity-/) | Small |
| Slow breathing in good sleepers (Laborde 2019) | n=64; sleep-quality score (PSQI) went from 3.31 to 2.91 vs control 3.44 to 3.75 [S20](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6406675/) | Small |
| One night of slow breathing (Kuula 2020) | n=20; time to fall asleep 12.56 vs 16.52 min, not significant (P = 0.40) [S21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7198497/) | No robust effect |
| Diaphragmatic breathing in older adults (Kakuei 2025) | 99 randomized, 74 analysed. Sleep quality improved (p < 0.001), but there was no significant reduction at 8 weeks. Trial quality score (PEDro) 5/10 [S22](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11325-025-03296-1), [S23](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/83417) | Low–moderate |
| Mindfulness improves sleep | Meta-analysis of 18 trials (1,654 participants): effect size 0.33 vs non-specific controls; no effect vs evidence-based treatments [S30](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6557693/). Black 2015 (n=49): PSQI 10.2 to 7.4 vs 10.2 to 9.1 [S29](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/600474) | Moderate |
| Longer exhale boosts relaxation (measured as HRV) | Mixed. At 6 breaths per minute, a 1:2 vs 1:1 inhale:exhale ratio made no HRV difference [S28](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11310264/) | Mixed |
| Cyclic sighing improves sleep | Mood improved, but sleep measures showed no change [S27](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/) | Not supported |
| 4-7-8 improves sleep | A trial is registered (n=82), but no results are posted [S33](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06103344). Any outcomes are UNVERIFIED | Unknown |
| Writing a to-do list helps you fall asleep faster | 57 students [S31](https://news.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2018/can-writing-your-dos-help-you-doze-baylor-study-suggests-jotting-down-tasks-can). The "9 minutes faster" figure is UNVERIFIED | Small |

## Building a bedtime routine

- Start the routine "anywhere between 30 minutes to 2 hours before bed". Put electronics away at the start of it ([S4](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/bedtime-routine-for-adults)).
- Avoid phones, tablets and computers for an hour before bed ([S8](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/how-to-fall-asleep-faster-and-sleep-better/)).
- Go to bed and get up at similar times every day ([S8](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/how-to-fall-asleep-faster-and-sleep-better/), [S6](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/)).
- Studies used 15–30 minutes a night for about 4 weeks ([S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41886931/), [S20](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6406675/)).
- "Repetitive and ongoing use... is usually more effective than one-time" ([S2](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/insomnia/treatment/relaxation-exercises-to-help-fall-asleep)).
- Lying or sitting are both fine ([S7](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/)). Cleveland Clinic suggests lying down while you learn ([S11](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/9445-diaphragmatic-breathing)).

## Waking up at night

- "Waking during the night is actually a normal part of sleep." Sleep cycles last 90–110 minutes ([S38](https://www.rte.ie/brainstorm/2026/0421/1569138-sleep-3am-insomnia-health/)).
- Use slow, deep breathing. Avoid clock-watching and screens ([S3](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/how-to-fall-back-asleep)).
- Get up after about 20 minutes ([S3](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/how-to-fall-back-asleep), [S8](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/how-to-fall-asleep-faster-and-sleep-better/)) or after 15 minutes ([S2](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/insomnia/treatment/relaxation-exercises-to-help-fall-asleep)). Do something relaxing elsewhere and go back to bed when sleepy.
- "the harder you try to sleep, the more difficult it is to fall asleep" ([S36](https://mielenterveystalo.fi/en/self-help/self-help-program-insomnia/7-paradoxical-intention-sos)).
- Frequent waking can be a sign of insomnia or sleep apnoea ([S13](https://newsroom.clevelandclinic.org/2024/02/16/why-youre-waking-up-at-3-a-m)).

## Safety and when to see a doctor

- **See a GP** if changing your habits has not helped, you have had trouble sleeping for months, or it affects daily life. Long-term insomnia means 3 months or longer ([S6](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/)).
- **CBT-I** is the first-line treatment and is usually 4–8 sessions ([S10](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/cognitive-behavioral-therapy-insomnia)). NHS says a GP may offer CBT face-to-face or as an online self-help programme ([S6](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/)).
- **Sleep apnoea warning signs:** breathing stopping and starting, gasping or choking, loud snoring, daytime tiredness, morning headaches. See a GP if you have these ([S9](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sleep-apnoea/)).
- **Who should check with a doctor before relaxation exercises:**
  - People with epilepsy, psychiatric conditions or a history of trauma ([S2](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/insomnia/treatment/relaxation-exercises-to-help-fall-asleep)).
  - People with respiratory problems or heart failure ([S15](https://www.harvardhealth.org/mind-and-mood/six-relaxation-techniques-to-reduce-stress)).
  - People with COPD, asthma or anxiety ([S11](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/9445-diaphragmatic-breathing)).
- **Side effects:** some people feel lightheaded with 4-7-8 ([S32](https://awcim.arizona.edu/content/CLH00048.html)). Focusing on the breath can raise anxiety in some people ([S37](https://www.icliniq.com/qa/insomnia/can-a-person-with-anxiety-and-insomnia-try-breathwork)).

## Common mistakes

- Expecting instant results ([S2](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/insomnia/treatment/relaxation-exercises-to-help-fall-asleep)).
- Forcing the breath ([S7](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/)).
- Long breath holds when they feel uncomfortable ([S2](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/insomnia/treatment/relaxation-exercises-to-help-fall-asleep)).
- Turning the exercise into an effort to *make* yourself sleep ([S36](https://mielenterveystalo.fi/en/self-help/self-help-program-insomnia/7-paradoxical-intention-sos)).
- Checking the clock ([S3](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/how-to-fall-back-asleep)).
- Ignoring insomnia that goes on for months, or apnoea signs ([S6](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/), [S9](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sleep-apnoea/)).

## Myths vs facts

- **Myth:** the military method makes you "fall asleep in 2 minutes".
  **Fact:** the "96 percent... two minutes" claim comes from Bud Winter's book, after six weeks of practice ([S34](https://www.mentalfloss.com/live-smarter/healthy-living/military-sleep-method)). We found no peer-reviewed test of it. The book's 1981 date is UNVERIFIED.
- **Myth:** you should fall asleep instantly.
  **Fact:** 10–20 minutes is normal. Falling asleep within a minute may suggest sleep deprivation ([S12](https://newsroom.clevelandclinic.org/2026/06/22/how-long-should-it-take-you-to-fall-asleep), [S5](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/how-sleep-works/sleep-latency)).
- **Myth:** breathing exercises cure insomnia.
  **Fact:** they are an adjunct (an add-on) ([S35](https://www.psychiatrictimes.com/view/controlled-breathing-exercises-adjunctive-treatment-for-insomnia)). CBT-I is the first-line treatment ([S16](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7853203/)).
- **Myth:** "the evidence is clear" ([S39](https://www.healthline.com/health/breathing-exercises-for-sleep)).
  **Fact:** the objective evidence is inconclusive ([S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41886931/)).

## What top-ranking pages cover and miss

Pages reviewed: Sleep Foundation ([S1](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/best-breathing-exercises-for-sleep)), Healthline ([S39](https://www.healthline.com/health/breathing-exercises-for-sleep)), Calm ([S40](https://www.calm.com/blog/breathing-exercises-for-sleep)), Sleep.com ([S41](https://www.sleep.com/sleep-health/breathing-exercises-for-sleep)) and Headspace ([S42](https://www.headspace.com/sleep/breathing-exercises-for-better-sleep)).

**What they cover:** lists of 5–9 techniques (4-7-8, belly breathing, box breathing, alternate nostril, Bhramari) with steps, plus routine tips.

**What they miss:**
- None grade how strong the evidence is.
- CBT-I is absent from four of the five pages.
- Sleep apnoea is absent from all five.
- None give the protocols used in research (about 6 breaths per minute, 15–30 minutes, about 4 weeks).
- 3am guidance is thin.
- None cover "sleep effort" or how breathing exercises can backfire.
- None mention that 10–20 minutes to fall asleep is normal.
- Some pages are dated: Healthline was last updated in 2022 and Sleep.com was published in 2022.
- Sleep.com and Headspace have no named medical reviewer.

**Phrasing that appears repeatedly:** "best breathing exercises for sleep", "breathing techniques for sleep", "fall asleep fast", "racing mind", "bedtime routine", "fall back asleep".
