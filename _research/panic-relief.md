## Summary
- Reputable sources agree that slow, gentle breathing helps during a panic attack. They disagree on whether the breath should be "deep". NHS and Cleveland Clinic say slowly and deeply but gently ([S1](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/), [S6](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/4451-panic-attack-panic-disorder)). Meuret's research group and Khazan warn that deep breaths can keep hyperventilation going ([S24](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3327286/), [S32](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/biofeedback-and-mindfulness-in-everyday-life/202007/3-steps-coping-panic-attack)). Top pages rarely explain this, so it is a clear content angle.
- Breathing exercises show modest benefit for stress and anxiety in general ([S20](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9828383/), [S21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8302658/)). For panic disorder specifically the evidence is weak or mixed ([S21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8302658/), [S27](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10883558/), [S28](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137372/)). CBT is the "gold standard" treatment ([S13](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms)).
- In the 2023 Stanford study, cyclic sighing improved mood more than mindfulness in healthy volunteers. State anxiety fell in all groups with no difference between them. People with panic disorder, and use during an attack, were not tested ([S17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)).
- Panic symptoms can feel like a heart attack ([S13](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms)). When in doubt, get emergency care ([S12](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/the-difference-between-panic-attacks-and-heart-attacks), [S16](https://www.healthline.com/health/panic-attack-vs-heart-attack)). Breathing into a paper bag has a documented risk of death ([S29](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2499228/)).
- The pages we found are mostly listicles with little physiology and weak or missing citations. They do not grade evidence or explain why breathing exercises sometimes backfire. Note that the breathing app Breathwrk does publish panic content ([S36](https://www.breathwrk.com/post/how-to-reduce-the-severity-of-a-panic-attack-by-breathing)), so the gap is versus pocketbreathcoach, not versus every app.

## What it is and why breathing helps
- **What a panic attack is.** NIMH: "A panic attack is a one-time or occasional episode of intense fear with physical and emotional symptoms." Panic disorder means frequent, unexpected attacks plus ongoing worry about having more ([S13](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms)).
- **Symptoms.** Pounding or racing heart, sweating, chills, trembling, difficulty breathing, weakness or dizziness, tingly or numb hands, chest pain, and stomach pain or nausea ([S13](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms)).
- **How long it lasts.** NHS: "Most panic attacks last between 5 and 20 minutes. Some have been reported to last up to an hour." ([S2](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/panic-disorder/)). HSE: "between 5 and 30 minutes" ([S3](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/panic-attacks/)). Cleveland Clinic: symptoms "usually peak within 10 minutes" ([S6](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/4451-panic-attack-panic-disorder)).
- **How common.** "Every year, up to 11% of people in the United States experience a panic attack" ([S6](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/4451-panic-attack-panic-disorder)). An estimated 2.7% of US adults had panic disorder in the past year and 4.7% at some point in their lives; past-year rates were 3.8% for females and 1.6% for males. These figures come from NCS-R data collected 2001–2003 ([S14](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/statistics/panic-disorder)).
- **Not dangerous.** NHS: "Although panic attacks are frightening, they're not dangerous." ([S2](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/panic-disorder/)).
- **Fight-or-flight.** "During a panic attack, your breathing speeds up, a signal that your body is in fight-or-flight mode." Slow breathing "helps to turn off the fight-or-flight response." (Regina Josell, PsyD, [S7](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/how-to-stop-a-panic-attack)).
- **Hyperventilation and CO2.** "Excessive breathing creates a low level of carbon dioxide in your blood" ([S15](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003071.htm)). Low CO2 narrows blood vessels, including the ones to the brain. It causes lightheadedness, tingling, chest pain and a pounding heart ([S8](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/symptoms/hyperventilation)), which overlap with panic symptoms. The home-care goal is "to raise the carbon dioxide level in your blood" ([S15](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003071.htm)). "Hypocapnia can produce unpleasant bodily sensations, the fear of which may contribute to panic." ([S24](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3327286/)). Meuret: "They feel like they're suffocating when in fact they feel that way because they have too much air." ([S26](https://www.smu.edu/News/2011/alicia-meuret-wsj-09feb2011)).
- **Vagus nerve and the parasympathetic system.**
  - Heart rate speeds up on the in-breath and slows on the out-breath. Vagal activity is "suppressed during inhalation and facilitated during exhalation and slow respiration cycles." The authors say the mechanisms are "proposed, not proven" ([S23](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6189422/)).
  - Slow breathing (<10 breaths/min) increased heart rate variability and reduced anxiety in healthy people. This review included 15 studies ([S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30245619/)).
  - Spiegel says exhalation activates the parasympathetic system, but his own trial saw no heart-rate change ([S18](https://med.stanford.edu/news/insights/2023/02/cyclic-sighing-can-help-breathe-away-anxiety.html)).

## Techniques and exact steps
- **NHS calming breath** ([S1](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/))
  1. Loosen tight clothes.
  2. "Let your breath flow as deep down into your belly as is comfortable, without forcing it."
  3. Breathe in through the nose and out through the mouth.
  4. Breathe in gently, optionally counting 1 to 5.
  5. Breathe out gently, counting 1 to 5.
  6. "Repeat for at least 5 minutes."
- **HSE** ([S3](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/panic-attacks/)): breathe in slowly, deeply and gently through the nose, and out the same way through the mouth. Count 1 to 5 on each breath.
- **Cleveland Clinic belly breathing** ([S7](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/how-to-stop-a-panic-attack))
  1. Sit or lie down if you can.
  2. Put one hand on your belly and one on your chest.
  3. Breathe slowly in through the nose and out through the mouth.
  4. The belly hand should move and the chest hand should stay still.
  5. Repeat "for several minutes or until you feel calm."
  - When helping someone else: "slowly count to five for each inhale and exhale" ([S6](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/4451-panic-attack-panic-disorder)).
- **Pursed-lip breathing**: "pucker your lips as if you're blowing out a candle and breathe out slowly" ([S8](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/symptoms/hyperventilation)). Healthline gives in for 2 counts, out for 4 or more ([S34](https://www.healthline.com/health/breathing-exercises-for-anxiety)).
- **Box breathing** ([S10](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)): breathe out fully, then in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. Do 3–4 rounds. Practise "one or two times a day, regardless of how you're feeling" and avoid straining.
  - Rethink's variant: in 4, hold 4, out 6, hold 2 ([S5](https://rethink.org/news-and-stories/commonly-asked-mental-health-questions/how/how-to-fight-a-panic-attack/)).
- **4-7-8** ([S9](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/4-7-8-breathing))
  - Rest your tongue tip behind your front teeth.
  - Breathe out with a whoosh, in through the nose for 4, hold for 7, then out through pursed lips for 8.
  - The steps say to repeat "for three more cycles", but the same page says "three cycles... twice a day". The page is inconsistent with itself.
  - Learners may feel lightheaded. The Dr. Weil limit of four breaths at first is UNVERIFIED.
- **Cyclic sighing** ([S17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/), [S18](https://med.stanford.edu/news/insights/2023/02/cyclic-sighing-can-help-breathe-away-anxiety.html))
  - Breathe in through the nose, then take a second, deeper "sip" of air to fill the lungs.
  - Breathe out very slowly through the mouth.
  - Repeat for about 5 minutes. The paper gives no timings in seconds.
  - Spiegel: "After one or two of these deep sighs, you may already feel calmer" (expert opinion; not tested during acute panic).
- **"Low and slow" / Hold-Breathe-Observe** (Inna Khazan, PhD, [S32](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/biofeedback-and-mindfulness-in-everyday-life/202007/3-steps-coping-panic-attack))
  - Pause the breath for about 10 seconds, 5–6 times.
  - Then take normal-sized belly breaths "(no need for a deep breath)" and exhale "as slowly as you comfortably can".
  - Then observe your senses.
- **CART (clinic-based, uses a CO2 monitor; not a do-it-yourself technique)** ([S24](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3327286/)): patients breathe "shallowly and regularly" to tones. The pace drops from 13 to 11, 9 and then 6 breaths/min over successive weeks, with a target PCO2 of 40 ±3 mm Hg.
- **Calming hand** (NHS trust leaflet): exact steps UNVERIFIED because the PDF could not be read ([S48](https://www.uhs.nhs.uk/Media/UHS-website-2019/Patientinformation/Medicinestherapiesandanaesthetics/Calming-hand-A-breathing-technique-for-breathlessness-3597-PIL.pdf)).
- **Grounding**
  - 5-4-3-2-1: 5 things you see, 4 you touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste. Don't "rely on grounding techniques alone" if anxiety is hard to manage ([S11](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/54321-grounding-technique), [S3](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/panic-attacks/)).
  - Rethink's 3-3-3: name 3 things you see, 3 you hear and 3 you touch ([S5](https://rethink.org/news-and-stories/commonly-asked-mental-health-questions/how/how-to-fight-a-panic-attack/)). A "move 3 body parts" version is UNVERIFIED (blog source only, [S42](https://damorementalhealth.com/what-is-the-3-3-3-rule-for-anxiety/)).
  - NHS also advises: stay where you are, remind yourself it will pass, and "remember it's not life threatening" ([S2](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/panic-disorder/)).
- **Where sources disagree**
  - Deep versus shallow breathing (see Summary).
  - Breath holds: used in box breathing, 4-7-8 and Khazan's method, but absent from the NHS and HSE methods.
  - Equal versus longer exhale: NHS, HSE and box use equal counts; 4-7-8, pursed-lip, Rethink and cyclic sighing use a longer exhale.
  - Stanford sample size: the paper reports 108 enrolled ([S17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)), while Stanford's press release says 111 ([S18](https://med.stanford.edu/news/insights/2023/02/cyclic-sighing-can-help-breathe-away-anxiety.html)).

## Benefits and evidence
| Claim | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Breathwork lowers self-reported stress and anxiety | Meta-analysis: 12 RCTs and 785 adults for stress, g = −0.35 [95% CI −0.55, −0.14]; anxiety g = −0.32. Most studies at moderate risk of bias. No clinical panic trials included ([S20](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9828383/)) | Moderate, but non-clinical samples |
| Respiratory interventions reduce anxiety | 40 RCTs and 3,047 participants, overall g = −0.678. Against active controls g = −0.279. Panic disorder subgroup (k = 4): g = −0.375, not significant ([S21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8302658/)) | Moderate for anxiety; weak for panic |
| Breathing retraining adds benefit within CBT for panic | 72 studies and 4,064 participants: "small effects on efficacy", though it improved acceptability ([S28](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137372/)). An RCT of 77 patients found results "generally more consistent with treatment equivalence" ([S27](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10883558/)). A review of 9 studies found them "not sufficiently compelling" ([S25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14531164/)) | Weak or mixed |
| CO2-guided shallow breathing (CART) reduces panic | RCT of 41 patients: improved as much as cognitive training. Only CART raised PCO2 ([S24](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3327286/)) | Small RCT |
| Cyclic sighing improves mood | 28 days, 5 min/day, healthy volunteers. Positive affect rose significantly more than with mindfulness. State anxiety fell in all groups with no difference between them. No HRV change. Exploratory and retrospectively registered ([S17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)) | Small RCT, non-clinical |
| Slow breathing raises HRV | Systematic review of 15 studies in healthy people ([S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30245619/)) | Moderate, mechanistic |
| 4-7-8 lowers heart rate and blood pressure; box breathing lowers cortisol | Claims made by Cleveland Clinic ([S9](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/4-7-8-breathing), [S10](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)); the underlying studies were not checked | UNVERIFIED |
| 5-4-3-2-1 grounding helps | Clinician recommendations only ([S11](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/54321-grounding-technique)) | Expert opinion; no trials found |

## Safety and when to seek help
- **Heart attack overlap.** Panic can bring "physical symptoms that might feel like a heart attack" ([S13](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms)). Signs that point to a heart attack ([S12](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/the-difference-between-panic-attacks-and-heart-attacks)):
  - pain spreading to the arm, jaw or neck
  - pressure or squeezing pain
  - symptoms after exertion
  - pain that "won't go away"
- **Don't wait it out.** "Don't wait to see if the symptoms go away" ([S12](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/the-difference-between-panic-attacks-and-heart-attacks)). "When in doubt... call emergency services right away" ([S16](https://www.healthline.com/health/panic-attack-vs-heart-attack)).
- **Emergency care.** Seek it for chest pain, trouble breathing or loss of consciousness ([S6](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/4451-panic-attack-panic-disorder)). Rapid breathing for the first time may be an emergency ([S15](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003071.htm)).
- **UK guidance.** If an attack lasts over 20 minutes, or comes with chest pain or an irregular heartbeat, call NHS 111. In an emergency, call 999 ([S5](https://rethink.org/news-and-stories/commonly-asked-mental-health-questions/how/how-to-fight-a-panic-attack/)).
- **Past heart attack.** Mayo advises urgent care for symptoms like a previous heart attack. UNVERIFIED: the page was blocked and this comes from a search snippet only ([S40](https://www.mayoclinic.org/connected-care/panic-attack-vs-heart-attack-how-to-tell-the-difference/cpt-20469325)).
- **Regular attacks.** See a GP ([S2](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/panic-disorder/), [S3](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/panic-attacks/)). Treatments include CBT and medication. For suicidal thoughts, call or text 988 (US) ([S13](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms)).
- **Medical causes of hyperventilation.** These include heart problems, lung disease, infection, ketoacidosis, bleeding and pregnancy ([S15](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003071.htm)).
- **Paper bags.** Avoid them, especially with heart or lung problems. This is University of Michigan advice reported second-hand by Mental Floss ([S30](https://www.mentalfloss.com/science/health/breathing-into-paper-bag-hyperventilating)).
- **Epilepsy.** Caution with fast or hyperventilation-style breathwork: UNVERIFIED (journal page blocked).
- **When relaxing makes it worse.** Relaxation-induced anxiety is documented in people with GAD and depression ([S31](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31450137/)). Lightheadedness is common when learning a technique ([S9](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/4-7-8-breathing)).

## Common mistakes
- **Gulping big, fast breaths.** Deep-breathing advice can lead to "compensatory deeper breathing, which in turn perpetuates hyperventilation and intensifies panic symptoms" ([S24](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3327286/), [S32](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/biofeedback-and-mindfulness-in-everyday-life/202007/3-steps-coping-panic-attack)).
- **Forcing or straining.** NHS says "without forcing it" ([S1](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/)); Cleveland Clinic says breathe "very gently and not straining" ([S10](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)).
- **Using a paper bag** ([S29](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2499228/)).
- **Only trying it mid-attack without practising.** NHS says you get the most benefit from regular practice ([S1](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/)).
- **Fighting the attack.** Rethink: "Try not to fight it." ([S5](https://rethink.org/news-and-stories/commonly-asked-mental-health-questions/how/how-to-fight-a-panic-attack/)).
- **Assuming chest pain is "just panic"** ([S12](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/the-difference-between-panic-attacks-and-heart-attacks)).

## Myths vs facts
- **Myth:** a paper bag helps. **Fact:** three deaths were reported in patients whose hypoxia or heart problems were missed. In 20 volunteers, oxygen fell by a mean maximal 26 mm Hg ([S29](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2499228/)). There is "a general lack of scientific evidence" that it works ([S30](https://www.mentalfloss.com/science/health/breathing-into-paper-bag-hyperventilating)).
- **Myth:** you can die from a panic attack. **Fact:** panic attacks are "not dangerous" ([S2](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/panic-disorder/), [S37](https://www.simplypsychology.org/can-you-die-from-a-panic-attack.html)). Rule out a heart attack when in doubt ([S12](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/the-difference-between-panic-attacks-and-heart-attacks)).
- **Myth:** breathlessness means you lack oxygen. **Fact:** the symptoms are caused by low CO2 from overbreathing ([S15](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003071.htm), [S26](https://www.smu.edu/News/2011/alicia-meuret-wsj-09feb2011)).
- **Myth:** always take a deep breath. **Fact:** sources disagree; slow and gentle matters more than deep ([S24](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3327286/), [S1](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/)).
- **Myth:** breathing exercises cure panic disorder. **Fact:** they add little within CBT ([S28](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137372/)). CBT is the "gold standard" ([S13](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms)).
- **Myth:** cyclic sighing is proven for panic. **Fact:** it was tested only in healthy volunteers ([S18](https://med.stanford.edu/news/insights/2023/02/cyclic-sighing-can-help-breathe-away-anxiety.html)).
