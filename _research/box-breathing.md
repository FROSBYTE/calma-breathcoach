## Summary

- **What it is:** Box breathing has four equal steps: breathe in, hold, breathe out, hold. Each step usually lasts a count of four. Other names for it are square breathing, four-square breathing, 4x4 or 4-4-4-4 breathing, equal breathing and *sama vritti pranayama* ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)).
- **Origin is murkier than most pages say:** The equal four-part breath with holds comes from yoga's *sama vritti* ([S37](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama_vritti)). A police trade magazine traces a matching four-count "Belly Breath" to a 1986 police book, and links it to Lt. Col. Dave Grossman's "Combat Breathing" ([S15](https://www.policemag.com/articles/box-breathing)). Former Navy SEAL Mark Divine popularised the name "box breathing". In his own TIME article he says he "developed" it "to continue my breath training after the SEALs" ([S14](https://time.com/4316151/breathing-technique-navy-seal-calm-focused/)). I found no official US Navy document saying SEALs train it (**UNVERIFIED**).
- **The evidence is small but positive for short-term stress and anxiety:**
  - In the 2023 Stanford trial, box breathing improved mood and lowered state anxiety within its own group. However, only cyclic sighing did significantly better than mindfulness meditation ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)).
  - A 2026 randomised trial with 66 participants found that box breathing blunted the rise in heart rate, anxiety and salivary alpha-amylase during a stress task ([S20](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1016/j.cpnec.2026.100360&resultType=core&format=json)).
  - A 2026 pilot study with 47 students found that one minute of box breathing reduced anxiety more than doing nothing ([S21](https://edoc.ku.de/id/eprint/36573/)).
- **Safety:** Medical pages call it "generally safe". They advise people with heart conditions, high blood pressure, a severe health condition, or pregnancy to check with a doctor first, and to stop if dizzy or breathless ([S7](https://www.healthline.com/health/box-breathing), [S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805)). The main trials excluded people with heart disease, pregnancy and moderate-to-severe psychiatric conditions ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)). So we don't know how it works for those groups.
- **Content gap:** I reviewed 8 pages that rank for "box breathing". None had an interactive guided timer, and only Healthline had any animation (a countdown GIF) ([S7](https://www.healthline.com/health/box-breathing)). Several misstate what the Stanford study found, or back up box-breathing claims with research on other techniques ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits), [S10](https://psychcentral.com/health/box-breathing)). Few explain how to adapt the breath holds.

## What it is and origin

### Definition
- Cleveland Clinic calls box breathing a yogic deep-breathing technique, also called *sama vritti pranayama*. The name comes from the four equal counts (inhale, hold, exhale, hold) that form the "sides" of a box ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)).
- WebMD: "Box breathing, also referred to as square breathing, is a deep breathing technique" ([S6](https://www.webmd.com/balance/what-is-box-breathing)).
- Healthline: "a deep breathing technique involving slow, deep breaths" ([S7](https://www.healthline.com/health/box-breathing)).

### The yoga root: sama vritti
- *Sama vritti* makes four phases of the breath equal in length ([S37](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama_vritti), [S38](https://www.yogaeasy.com/artikel/how-to-practice-samavritti-breath)):
  - inhalation (*puraka*)
  - hold after the inhale (*antara kumbhaka*)
  - exhalation (*rechaka*)
  - hold after the exhale (*bahya kumbhaka*)
- The Wikipedia entry suggests starting with unequal breathing and shorter holds, then working up to equal durations ([S37](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama_vritti)).

## Exact steps and timings

### Step-by-step (common to reputable sources)
Steps and setup are combined from Cleveland Clinic ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)), Healthline ([S7](https://www.healthline.com/health/box-breathing)), WebMD ([S6](https://www.webmd.com/balance/what-is-box-breathing)), MNT ([S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805)) and Divine/TIME ([S14](https://time.com/4316151/breathing-technique-navy-seal-calm-focused/)).

**Getting set up**
- Sit upright with your back supported and feet flat on the floor ([S7](https://www.healthline.com/health/box-breathing), [S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805)).
- WebMD also allows standing or lying on your back ([S6](https://www.webmd.com/balance/what-is-box-breathing)).
- Optional: place one hand on your chest and one on your stomach to check you're breathing into the belly ([S6](https://www.webmd.com/balance/what-is-box-breathing), [S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805)).

**The steps**
1. Breathe out slowly and fully to empty your lungs ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits), [S7](https://www.healthline.com/health/box-breathing)).
2. Breathe in through your nose for a count of 4 ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)).
3. Hold for a count of 4. MNT says to do this "without clamping the mouth or nose" ([S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805)). Divine says "do not clamp down and create back pressure" ([S14](https://time.com/4316151/breathing-technique-navy-seal-calm-focused/)).
4. Breathe out for a count of 4 ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)).
5. Hold with empty lungs for a count of 4, then repeat ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)).

**Where sources differ**
- **Exhale through the nose or the mouth?** Healthline says out through the mouth ([S7](https://www.healthline.com/health/box-breathing)). Divine says "exhale smoothly through your nose" ([S14](https://time.com/4316151/breathing-technique-navy-seal-calm-focused/)). The Stanford trial told people to breathe "preferably through the nose" ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)). Cleveland Clinic doesn't specify ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)).
- **Where to start:** Divine starts with an empty-lung hold after breathing out fully ([S14](https://time.com/4316151/breathing-technique-navy-seal-calm-focused/)). Most other sources start the count on the inhale ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits), [S7](https://www.healthline.com/health/box-breathing)).

**Evidence on how much to do:** A 2023 systematic review of 58 breathing studies found that "Six of nine" sessions under 5 minutes were ineffective. Multiple sessions were used in 50 of 54 effective interventions. The authors state that "any session duration beyond 5 min can be effective" ([S27](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10741869/)). This review covers breathing practices in general, not box breathing alone.

**Note the conflict for copy:** Guidance ranges from "3–5 cycles at a time" to "10–20 minutes a day". The copy should present the shorter amount as the place to start and treat longer sessions as optional.

### Variants of the count
- **2 or 3 seconds per step for beginners** ([S6](https://www.webmd.com/balance/what-is-box-breathing), [S10](https://psychcentral.com/health/box-breathing)).
- **3-3-3-3:** "may suit some people better" ([S15](https://www.policemag.com/articles/box-breathing)). Count to three if four is too hard ([S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805)).
- **5 or 6 per step** as you progress ([S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805)). Cleveland Clinic also says you can lengthen the count over time ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)).
- **5 seconds per stage** is attributed to Divine's book by a book-summary site ([S56](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=85467)). **UNVERIFIED** against the book itself.
- **Personalised timing (Stanford trial):**
  - Before starting, participants timed how long they could slowly breathe out after a full breath (a "CO2 discard" test).
  - Their phase length then followed the study's lookup table ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)):
    - "0–20 s C02 discard time = your inhales, exhales, and breath holds should be 3 - 4s."
    - "25–45 s ... 5 - 6s."
    - "50 - 75 + sec ... 8–10 s."
  - The table leaves 21–24 s and 46–49 s undefined.
- **Longer exhale variant:** Police Magazine suggests extending the exhale to 6–8 seconds if you like ([S15](https://www.policemag.com/articles/box-breathing)). This is no longer strictly "box".
- **Yoga variants:** Keep the inhale and exhale at 4 and shorten the holds (e.g. to 2). Or drop the holds entirely if they are uncomfortable ([S38](https://www.yogaeasy.com/artikel/how-to-practice-samavritti-breath)).

### Breathing rate (my arithmetic, not from a source)
| Pattern | One cycle | Breaths per minute |
|---|---|---|
| 3-3-3-3 | 12 s | 5 |
| 4-4-4-4 | 16 s | 3.75 |
| 5-5-5-5 | 20 s | 3 |

For comparison:
- A physiology review defines "slow breathing as any rate from 4 to 10 breaths per min", against a typical 10–20 breaths a minute ([S29](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5709795/)).
- Heart-rate-variability biofeedback research centres on about 6 breaths a minute (0.1 Hz) ([S30](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4104929/)).
- So standard 4-4-4-4 box breathing is slower than both.

## How it works

Plain-language explanation for the copywriter, with the strength of each claim noted.

1. **Slow breathing shifts the nervous system toward "rest and digest".**
   - Cleveland Clinic says box breathing activates the parasympathetic nervous system, moving the body out of "fight or flight" ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)).
   - A systematic review of 15 studies found slow breathing (<10 breaths/min) linked to higher heart rate variability and respiratory sinus arrhythmia. Reported psychological effects included more comfort and relaxation and less anxiety, depression and anger ([S28](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137615)).
   - *Strength: review of small studies in healthy people. The review itself says the mechanisms are "still under debate"* ([S28](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137615)).
2. **Heart rate rises and falls with each breath.**
   - "the heart rate increases during inspiration while arterial blood pressure decreases, and vice versa during expiration" ([S29](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5709795/)).
   - Slow breathing "increases amplitudes of blood pressure oscillations and HRV" ([S29](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5709795/)).
   - Stanford psychiatrist David Spiegel: "Exhalation activates the parasympathetic nervous system" ([S2](https://med.stanford.edu/news/insights/2023/02/cyclic-sighing-can-help-breathe-away-anxiety.html)).
   - *Strength: established physiology for slow breathing generally.*
3. **Breath holds briefly raise carbon dioxide.**
   - Pulmonologist Dr. Ni-Cheng Liang (AHA News): "When you breath-hold, that increases your carbon dioxide level temporarily." She continues: "when you increase your carbon dioxide level in your bloodstream, that decreases your heart rate" ([S16](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2023-07-inspirationcareful-health.html)).
   - *Strength: expert opinion. I found no box-breathing study that measured CO2.*
4. **Counting gives the mind a focus.**
   - Cleveland Clinic says silently counting works like mantra meditation and encourages mindfulness ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)).
   - *Strength: expert opinion.*
5. **"Neutral" energy effect.**
   - Divine: "Box breathing with this four-four-four-four ratio has a neutral energetic effect." He says it leaves you alert and grounded, neither energised nor sleepy ([S14](https://time.com/4316151/breathing-technique-navy-seal-calm-focused/)).
   - *Strength: personal opinion. Not tested.*

**Important caveats for the copy**
- **Heart rate variability:** Neither of the two most relevant box-breathing trials found an HRV change.
  - In the Stanford trial, resting heart rate and HRV (RMSSD) did not change significantly in any group ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)).
  - In McAllister 2026, "Heart rate variability, cortisol, and cognitive performance did not differ across the three conditions" ([S20](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1016/j.cpnec.2026.100360&resultType=core&format=json)). HRV there was measured at rest before and after, not during the breathing ([S19](https://www.psypost.org/brief-breathing-exercises-blunt-the-body-s-physical-reaction-to-acute-stress/)).
  - **Do not claim box breathing "boosts HRV."**
- **"CO2 tolerance":** The claim that box breathing "builds CO2 tolerance" over time is **UNVERIFIED**. The Stanford trial used an exhale test only to set each person's pace ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)). It did not measure whether CO2 tolerance changed.

## Benefits and evidence

### Box-breathing-specific studies

| Benefit claimed | What the study found (as reported) | Design and size | Evidence strength | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Better mood | Daily positive affect rose 1.84 ± 3.24 (p = 0.026) in the box group. WebMD states "Lowers blood pressure and decreases Cortisol" without a citation ([S6](https://www.webmd.com/balance/what-is-box-breathing)). In McAllister 2026, cortisol fell in all groups with no difference between them ([S19](https://www.psypost.org/brief-breathing-exercises-blunt-the-body-s-physical-reaction-to-acute-stress/)). | n/a | **UNVERIFIED** for box breathing | as listed |
| Better sleep | **No box-breathing sleep trial found.** In the Stanford trial, there were no significant changes in sleep hours, sleep efficiency, sleep score or daytime sleepiness ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)). WebMD's "helps with sleep during insomnia" is uncited ([S6](https://www.webmd.com/balance/what-is-box-breathing)). | n/a | **UNVERIFIED** | as listed |

### The 2023 Stanford study, reported accurately
Balban MY et al., "Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal," *Cell Reports Medicine*, 2023 ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)).

**Design**
- Remote randomised controlled trial (NCT05304000), registered retrospectively.
- 28 days of 5 minutes a day.
- 108 participants were randomised:
  - 24 to mindfulness meditation
  - 30 to cyclic sighing
  - 21 to box breathing
  - 33 to cyclic hyperventilation with retention
- The Stanford news story gives "111 healthy volunteers" ([S2](https://med.stanford.edu/news/insights/2023/02/cyclic-sighing-can-help-breathe-away-anxiety.html)). This differs from the paper's figure.

**Who was excluded:** People with moderate to severe psychiatric or medical conditions that participation could worsen, e.g. heart disease, glaucoma, seizures, pregnancy, psychosis, bipolar disorder ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)).

**Box breathing protocol:** Equal inhale, hold, exhale and hold, with the length set by the CO2 discard lookup table (3–10 s per phase) ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)).

**Key results (as written)** ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/))
- **Positive affect:** Breathwork as a whole beat mindfulness meditation. Only cyclic sighing differed significantly from mindfulness. The authors: "The other two breathwork groups were also higher than mindfulness meditation; however, this difference was not significant."
- **Negative affect and state anxiety:** All groups improved (p < 0.0001), with no between-group differences.
- **Respiratory rate:** Combined breathwork lowered it more than mindfulness, and cyclic sighing did so significantly. No separate box-breathing result was reported.
- **Heart rate and HRV:** No significant change in any group.
- **Sleep:** No significant changes.
- **Highlights:** "Cyclic sighing is most effective at improving mood and reducing respiratory rate."

**Limitations stated by the authors** ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/))
- It was an exploratory study.
- Remote design with self-reported adherence.
- Only 4 weeks, with no follow-up.
- Small group sizes "limit[ed] the statistical power to compare individual breathwork groups with each other".

**Conflict of interest:** Co-author Andrew Huberman became an advisor to WHOOP, whose wristbands were used in the study ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/), [S2](https://med.stanford.edu/news/insights/2023/02/cyclic-sighing-can-help-breathe-away-anxiety.html)).

**Outside comment:** Stanford's own story quotes outside researcher Sara Lazar calling it "just one study with a pretty small sample size" (this quote came via a search-result summary of the Stanford coverage, **UNVERIFIED** wording). The story itself describes the trial as preliminary, in healthy volunteers ([S2](https://med.stanford.edu/news/insights/2023/02/cyclic-sighing-can-help-breathe-away-anxiety.html)).

**What copy can safely say:** "In a 2023 Stanford-led trial, five minutes a day of box breathing was linked with better mood and less anxiety over a month. A different technique, cyclic sighing (a double inhale followed by a long exhale), showed the strongest effects."

### Breathwork in general (context, not box-specific)
- **2023 meta-analysis (Fincham et al., *Scientific Reports*)** ([S26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9828383/)):
  - **Stress:** 12 RCTs, 785 adults, g = −0.35 [95% CI −0.55, −0.14] in favour of breathwork.
  - **Anxiety:** "g = − 0.32 [95% CI − 0.48, − 0.16]" (k = 20).
  - **Depression:** "g = − 0.40 [95% CI − 0.58, − 0.22]" (k = 18).
  - **By population:** The effect on stress was significant in non-clinical samples, but not in mental or physical health populations. The authors say "it could be premature to recommend breathwork in these contexts".
  - **Authors' caution:** "we urge caution and advocate for nuanced research approaches with low risk-of-bias study designs".
  - Box breathing was not mentioned.
- **2023 systematic review (Bentley et al., *Brain Sciences*)** ([S27](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10741869/)):
  - 58 studies; "Fifty-four of the studies' 72 interventions were effective."
  - Fast-only breathing and sessions under 5 minutes were flagged to avoid.
  - Human-guided training, multiple sessions and long-term practice ("≥6 sessions over ≥1 week") were linked to effectiveness.

## Safety

**General**
- Healthline calls box breathing "generally safe" ([S7](https://www.healthline.com/health/box-breathing)).
- If you feel breathless or dizzy, stop and go back to normal breathing. Get medical attention if symptoms don't go away ([S7](https://www.healthline.com/health/box-breathing)).
- Breathe gently, don't strain, and keep a comfortable pace, "not too fast or too slow" (paraphrase) ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)).

**Who should check with a doctor first**

| Group | Guidance | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Heart condition, a severe health condition, or pregnancy | Check with your doctor first | [S7](https://www.healthline.com/health/box-breathing) |
| High blood pressure, or pregnancy | Consult a doctor first | [S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805) |
| Heart, lung or mental health condition | Check with a health care professional (Dr. Liang, AHA News) | [S16](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2023-07-inspirationcareful-health.html) |
| Prone to lightheadedness; respiratory or cardiovascular conditions | Box breathing "may not be appropriate for everyone, especially for someone prone to lightheadedness" | [S17](https://www.healio.com/news/primary-care/20261013/box-breathing-helps-manage-stress) |
| Conditions that affect breath-holding, "such as respiratory issues or anxiety disorders" | Consult a healthcare professional. If holds are hard, shorten or drop them. | [S12](https://dhwblog.dukehealth.org/mastering-box-breathing-a-simple-technique-to-relieve-stress/) |
| People who find breath-holding difficult | "may not be suited" to box breathing | [S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805) |

**Yoga-teacher cautions for sama vritti.** These are lower-authority but specific ([S38](https://www.yogaeasy.com/artikel/how-to-practice-samavritti-breath)):
- "If you are pregnant, practise equalizing the inhale and exhale without the retentions."
- "If you have high blood pressure, lung, heart, eye or ear problems it's advised not to hold the breath after the inhale."
- "If you have low blood pressure it's advised not to hold the breath after the exhale."
- "Never strain the breath in Pranayama."

**Evidence gap**
- The Stanford trial excluded people with heart disease, glaucoma, seizures, pregnancy, psychosis, bipolar disorder and other moderate-to-severe conditions ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)).
- Most other studies used healthy students ([S19](https://www.psypost.org/brief-breathing-exercises-blunt-the-body-s-physical-reaction-to-acute-stress/), [S21](https://edoc.ku.de/id/eprint/36573/), [S23](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0336615)).
- The breathwork meta-analysis says clinicians should consider whether breathwork may worsen symptoms of some conditions ([S26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9828383/)).
- So we have little safety evidence for these higher-risk groups.

**Panic-prone people**
- Research shows "stronger reactivity evident in panic disorder (PD) or panic attacks as compared to nonclinical control participants" when people breathe CO2-enriched air ([S36](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502686/)).
- Breath holds raise CO2 temporarily ([S16](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2023-07-inspirationcareful-health.html)).
- Putting these together, holds *may* feel uncomfortable for panic-prone people. That is my inference; I found no study testing box breathing in people with panic disorder (**UNVERIFIED**).
- Safe copy: "If the holds make you feel more anxious or short of breath, shorten them or skip them and just breathe slowly."

**Dizziness**
- Dr. Liang notes beginners may get dizzy, and advises 3–5 cycles at a time ([S16](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2023-07-inspirationcareful-health.html)).

**Not a treatment**
- No source presents box breathing as a treatment for anxiety disorders, COPD or high blood pressure.
- Cleveland Clinic says diaphragmatic breathing "shouldn't be the only treatment" for conditions such as anxiety, asthma and COPD ([S5](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/9445-diaphragmatic-breathing)). Apply the same framing to box breathing.

## Common mistakes and tips

**Common mistakes**

| Mistake | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Clamping the throat or nose shut during holds | "do not clamp down and create back pressure" ([S14](https://time.com/4316151/breathing-technique-navy-seal-calm-focused/)). Hold "without clamping the mouth or nose" ([S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805)). | S9, S14 |
| Straining or forcing the breath | Keep it gentle and at your comfort level ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)). The NHS says breathe in "without forcing it" ([S34](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/)). | S3, S34 |
| Starting with counts that are too long | Start at 2–3 seconds ([S6](https://www.webmd.com/balance/what-is-box-breathing)) or 3-3-3-3 ([S15](https://www.policemag.com/articles/box-breathing)) | S6, S15 |
| Doing too many rounds at first and getting dizzy | 3–5 cycles at a time ([S16](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2023-07-inspirationcareful-health.html)); 3–4 rounds ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)) | S3, S16 |
| Shallow chest breathing | Put one hand on your chest and one on your stomach; the stomach should move ([S6](https://www.webmd.com/balance/what-is-box-breathing)). Fill the lungs "from bottom to top" (paraphrase) ([S15](https://www.policemag.com/articles/box-breathing)). | S6, S15 |
| Forcing the belly out | "Do not force the abdominal muscles outward" (paraphrase) | [S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805) |
| Pushing through uncomfortable holds | Shorten or drop the holds and focus on slow, steady breathing | [S12](https://dhwblog.dukehealth.org/mastering-box-breathing-a-simple-technique-to-relieve-stress/), [S38](https://www.yogaeasy.com/artikel/how-to-practice-samavritti-breath) |
| Expecting to master it right away | Don't pressure yourself to master it right away (paraphrase) | [S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits) |
| Only using it in a crisis | Practise daily when calm too | [S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits), [S34](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/) |

**Tips**
- **Visualise or follow a box.**
  - Picture tracing a square ([S11](https://www.onepeloton.com/blog/box-breathing-technique)). The name exists to prompt people to visualise a box ([S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805)).
  - In the McAllister trial, participants followed a visual timer on screen ([S19](https://www.psypost.org/brief-breathing-exercises-blunt-the-body-s-physical-reaction-to-acute-stress/)).
  - Whether a visual guide improves results compared with counting in your head is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Choose a quiet spot.** A quiet, dimly lit room is optional but can help you focus ([S7](https://www.healthline.com/health/box-breathing)). Box breathing can also be done almost anywhere ([S10](https://psychcentral.com/health/box-breathing)).
- **Build up gradually.** Start with about 20 seconds and build toward a minute or longer ([S11](https://www.onepeloton.com/blog/box-breathing-technique)). Lengthen the count over time ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)).
- **Use it as a warm-up.** Do box breathing first, then mindfulness or meditation ([S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805)).
- **Stack it onto an existing routine** ([S11](https://www.onepeloton.com/blog/box-breathing-technique)).
- **Repeat over days.** Repeated sessions over at least a week were linked to effectiveness in breathing studies generally ([S27](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10741869/)).

## Comparisons

### Box breathing vs 4-7-8

| | Box breathing | 4-7-8 |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern | In 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4 ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)) | In through the nose 4, hold 7, out through the mouth 8, with the tongue tip behind the upper front teeth ([S4](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/4-7-8-breathing)) |
| Holds | Two (full and empty lungs) | One (full lungs) |
| Exhale | Same length as the inhale | Twice as long as the inhale |
| Popularised by | Mark Divine ([S15](https://www.policemag.com/articles/box-breathing)) | "popularized in 2015 by integrative medicine specialist Andrew Weil, MD", rooted in pranayama ([S4](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/4-7-8-breathing)) |
| Typical dose | 3–4 rounds ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)) | Four cycles; Dr. Young suggests three cycles twice a day ([S4](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/4-7-8-breathing)) |
| Expert-suggested use | Staying focused and alert but calm, e.g. before a test or speech ([S16](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2023-07-inspirationcareful-health.html)) | For feeling stressed or anxious, or having trouble winding down or falling asleep, for anyone without chronic lung disease ([S16](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2023-07-inspirationcareful-health.html)). Also "can help you fall asleep" ([S4](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/4-7-8-breathing)). |
| Evidence | Small RCTs and a pilot (see Benefits) | One small study: 43 adults aged 19–25. "Both groups had significantly decreased HR and systolic BP (p < 0.05)." The authors say it "can help participants improve their HRV and BP, particularly in those without sleep deprivation" ([S31](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9277512/)). |
| Side effects | Dizziness for beginners ([S16](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2023-07-inspirationcareful-health.html)) | Beginners may feel lightheaded or short of breath ([S4](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/4-7-8-breathing), [S8](https://www.healthline.com/health/4-7-8-breathing)) |

- **Head-to-head evidence:** I found no trial comparing box breathing directly with 4-7-8. "Which is better" claims are **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Blog claims:** Comparison blogs often say "box for daytime, 4-7-8 for sleep" ([S48](https://longevity.technology/news/box-breathing-vs-4-7-8-which-technique-is-better/)). That is opinion, not trial evidence.

### Box breathing vs diaphragmatic (belly) breathing
- **They describe different things.** Diaphragmatic breathing is about *how* you breathe: the belly rises while the chest stays still, and you breathe out through pursed lips ([S5](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/9445-diaphragmatic-breathing)). Box breathing is about *timing*. Many box-breathing guides tell you to breathe into the belly as well ([S6](https://www.webmd.com/balance/what-is-box-breathing), [S15](https://www.policemag.com/articles/box-breathing)).
- **Dose:** Cleveland Clinic: "At first, practice this exercise for five to 10 minutes about three to four times per day." ([S5](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/9445-diaphragmatic-breathing))
- **Evidence:**
  - A 2019 systematic review found only 3 eligible studies. It concluded diaphragmatic breathing "may decrease stress" on both biomarkers and self-report ([S33](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31436595/)).
  - A 40-person RCT used 20 sessions over 8 weeks at about 4 breaths/min with a feedback device. It reported improved sustained attention, lower negative affect and lower cortisol ([S32](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5455070/)).
- **Why this matters for copy:** Several top box-breathing pages cite these diaphragmatic-breathing papers as if they were box-breathing evidence ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits), [S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805), [S10](https://psychcentral.com/health/box-breathing)). Our page should not.

### Box breathing vs cyclic sighing
- **Cyclic sighing technique:** Two inhales through the nose, the second a short top-up, then a slow, full exhale, ideally through the mouth. There are no holds ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)).
- **Stanford 2023 trial** ([S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)):
  - Cyclic sighing was the only technique that improved positive mood significantly more than mindfulness.
  - It was also the only one that cut respiratory rate significantly more than mindfulness.
  - Box breathing and cyclic sighing both reduced anxiety, with no between-group differences.
  - The authors attribute sighing's edge to "(1) extended exhalation and (2) the double inhale".
- **Riedl 2026 pilot:** One minute of either technique reduced state anxiety more than control. Only box breathing was followed by fewer attention errors ([S21](https://edoc.ku.de/id/eprint/36573/)). From the abstract, I could not verify any direct statistical comparison between the two.

### Box breathing vs prolonged-exhale breathing
- **McAllister 2026:** The prolonged-exhale group breathed in for 3 s and out for 6 s through pursed lips, once every 30 s. Both techniques equally buffered the stress response ([S19](https://www.psypost.org/brief-breathing-exercises-blunt-the-body-s-physical-reaction-to-acute-stress/), [S20](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1016/j.cpnec.2026.100360&resultType=core&format=json)).
- **Röttger 2021:** Tactical breathing gave lower physiological arousal, while prolonged exhalation gave better task performance ([S18](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:32757097%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json)).

### Box breathing vs the NHS calm-breathing exercise
- The NHS stress exercise has no holds. You breathe in gently, optionally counting 1 to 5, and breathe out the same way, for "at least 5 minutes" ([S34](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/)).
- This is a useful no-holds alternative to suggest for people who dislike breath holds.

**What they all cover**
- The 4-4-4-4 steps

**Gaps (opportunities)**
1. **No guided, follow-along pacer.** None of the 8 pages has an interactive timer or full animated guide. Healthline's GIF covers only one step ([S7](https://www.healthline.com/health/box-breathing)). Demand for animated guides is visible in the many dedicated box-breathing apps in the App Store, e.g. [S54](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6590601748). A trial also used an on-screen visual timer to pace participants ([S19](https://www.psypost.org/brief-breathing-exercises-blunt-the-body-s-physical-reaction-to-acute-stress/)).
2. **Stanford study misreported or oversimplified.** Psych Central and Peloton cite it for box breathing's benefits but don't mention that cyclic sighing outperformed it ([S10](https://psychcentral.com/health/box-breathing), [S11](https://www.onepeloton.com/blog/box-breathing-technique), [S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)).
3. **Borrowed evidence.** Cortisol, blood-pressure and attention claims are backed by diaphragmatic-breathing or general relaxation research ([S3](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits), [S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805), [S10](https://psychcentral.com/health/box-breathing)).
4. **Newest box-specific research is missing.** None of the pages reviewed covers McAllister 2026, Riedl 2026 or Kasap 2025 ([S20](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1016/j.cpnec.2026.100360&resultType=core&format=json), [S21](https://edoc.ku.de/id/eprint/36573/), [S23](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0336615)).
5. **Thin or missing safety content.** Psych Central, Psychology Today and WebMD have no safety cautions. Only Duke explains what to do if holds are hard ([S12](https://dhwblog.dukehealth.org/mastering-box-breathing-a-simple-technique-to-relieve-stress/)).
6. **Origin nuance is missing.** No page explains the yoga, police (1986) and Grossman lineage together. None notes that Divine says he developed it after leaving the SEALs ([S14](https://time.com/4316151/breathing-technique-navy-seal-calm-focused/), [S15](https://www.policemag.com/articles/box-breathing)).
7. **Conflicting dose advice goes unexplained.** "3–5 cycles" vs "10–20 minutes" is never reconciled ([S16](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2023-07-inspirationcareful-health.html), [S14](https://time.com/4316151/breathing-technique-navy-seal-calm-focused/)).
8. **Few or no FAQs.** Only MNT has an FAQ, with 2 questions ([S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805)).
9. **Comparisons are shallow.** None of the pages reviewed compares box breathing with cyclic sighing. Only Psych Central and MNT list other techniques ([S10](https://psychcentral.com/health/box-breathing), [S9](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321805)).
10. **No guidance on when *not* to use it,** such as straight after intense exercise ([S23](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0336615)).
